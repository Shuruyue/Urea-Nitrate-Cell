Model

kinetics: j = 10 (10η/b − 1) / (10η₁₀/b − 1) mA cm⁻², anchored at the potential E₁₀ where j = 10 mA cm⁻²
nitrate: jNO₃ = jk jlim / (jk + jlim),   jk ∝ (C/100 mM)γ,   jlim = 8FDC/δ (Koutecký–Levich)
full cell: jUOR(Ea) + jOER(Ea) = j,   jNO₃(Ec) + jHER(Ec) = j,   Vcell = Ec − Ea − jRΩ
batch: dC/dt = −FENO₃ I / (8 F V), explicit Euler with 10 s steps until 90 % is removed

Against the data, the electrode potential seen by the potentiostat includes the uncompensated resistance Ru (and a background current at the cathode), both fitted in calibration.py; the curves on the data tab use those fitted values with the parameters on the left.

Checks

The JavaScript model of this page is a line-by-line copy of urea_nitrate_cell/model.py and of the measured-curve functions of calibration.py. scripts/verify_web_page.py runs it in Node.js for random parameter sets and compares cell voltages, electrode potentials, Faradaic efficiencies, limiting currents, measured LSV currents and whole batch runs with the Python package; the test (part of the pytest suite) passes only if they agree to a relative 10⁻⁸. The model itself is checked by the 36 validation checks of the repository; the fit to the LSV data is reported there as metrics, without a pass/fail threshold.

Calibration is not independent validation: S1 is fitted to the same LSV curves, and no full-cell data exist yet to test the absolute cell voltage. S2, S3 and RΩ are assumptions.

Limitations and sources

Single Tafel slopes, a fixed diffusion layer, HER kinetics unaffected by nitrate; no by-products (nitrite, urea over-oxidation), pH drift or membrane potentials; urea is taken to be in excess at the anode.

Data: LSV curves digitized from M. Yao et al., Sustainability 17, 3835 (2025), CC BY 4.0. Thermodynamics: NBS tables (Wagman et al., 1982). Nitrate diffusion coefficient: CRC Handbook of Chemistry and Physics.
